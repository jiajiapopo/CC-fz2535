## Audiovisual Instrument

For Week 10 I chose Option 1: a performable audiovisual instrument. This README is mostly a record of the problems that came up along the way and how I improved things.

The idea of counting fingers on the left hand to choose a chord came from a Mac app I tried called Airmony.

At first, the height of my index fingertip set the pitch, and the distance between my thumb and index finger set the volume. But when I pinched my fingers to make the sound quieter, my fingertip moved down, and the pitch dropped with it. So I changed it: the distance between thumb and index finger sets the pitch, and the volume is simply fixed. I also set the left hand so that tilting it inward switches to minor. Later I found the tilt was too easy to trigger by accident, so I changed it to need a more exaggerated tilt.

Then balloons started floating across the picture. macOS has a "Reactions" feature that adds effects automatically when it sees certain gestures. A peace sign makes balloons, and that is exactly my gesture for the II chord.

Next came the fine-tuning. The way the left hand played its arpeggio gave me a lot of trouble. I tried different patterns and rhythms, but I was still not happy with it.

For the melody in the right hand, I planned to use the pinch distance to play the four chord tones. That turned out a bit monotonous, so I changed it to a seven-note scale. But the distance was too hard to control. It needed very fine control, and moving my fingers set off other notes on the way.

After that I changed the right hand completely, to playing by touching a grid of boxes on the screen. The result was that every box my finger passed over made a sound. So I tried to add a "click" gesture, but the camera does not seem to be good at recognizing a clicking movement of the hand.

In the end I decided to go back to pinching chord tones, and changed the left hand from an arpeggio to striking the whole chord on every beat. This time I found a better way for the right hand: each of the four fingers other than the thumb pinches against the thumb in turn, and they match the four tones of the seventh chord. This is much easier to control. The only drawback I found after practicing is that pinching the pinky and thumb together often makes my hand sore and cramped.

### How to play

Left hand: one, two, three, four, or five fingers select chords I to V in the key of C. Thumb and pinky together select the VI chord, and adding the index finger selects the VII chord. Tilting the hand inward switches to minor.

Right hand: each of the four fingers other than the thumb pinches against the thumb to play one of the four tones of the seventh chord. Moving the hand into the high, middle, or low zone of the screen selects one of three octaves. Opening the hand completely silences it.
